What a European invoice must include

Article 226 of the EU VAT Directive (2006/112/EC) sets out the particulars every VAT invoice must show. This generator covers all of them:

  • A unique, sequential invoice number
  • The date of issue
  • Your full name and address
  • Your VAT identification number
  • Your client's full name and address
  • Client's VAT ID for cross-border B2B supplies
  • Description of the goods or services
  • Quantity and unit price for each item
  • The VAT rate applied and the VAT amount
  • Net, VAT and gross totals
  • Reverse-charge note where it applies
  • Exemption reference for small businesses

This tool helps you prepare a correctly formatted invoice; it is not tax advice. VAT rules vary by country, sector and customer type — for anything unusual, confirm with your accountant or tax office.

Standard VAT rates in the EU (2026)

Every EU member state sets its own standard VAT rate. When you select your country in the tool, its standard rate is filled in for you — reduced rates for specific goods and services can be entered as a custom rate.

For comparison: the United Kingdom applies 20%, Switzerland 8.1% and Norway 25%. The United States has no VAT (state sales taxes may apply instead).

Frequently asked questions

Is this invoice generator really free?

Yes — completely. There is no sign-up, no trial and no watermark on the PDF. The whole tool runs in your browser, so your business and client data never leaves your device.

What is the reverse charge, and when do I use it?

If you supply services to a business customer in another EU country, you normally do not charge VAT. Instead, your customer declares the VAT in their own country — the "reverse charge" under Article 196 of the EU VAT Directive. Your invoice shows 0% VAT and must carry a reverse-charge note, which this tool adds automatically when you select that option. You will need your customer's valid VAT ID.

What is the small-business exemption?

Many EU countries let very small businesses skip charging VAT under a national scheme — Germany's §19 UStG (Kleinunternehmerregelung) is the best-known example, with equivalents in France, Austria, the Netherlands and elsewhere. If you are registered under such a scheme, select "Small-business exemption": the invoice shows 0% VAT with the exemption stated, as the law requires.

Which VAT rate should I charge a client in another EU country?

For B2B services, the reverse charge usually applies (0% on your invoice). For B2C digital services sold across EU borders, VAT is due at the customer's country rate, normally declared through the One Stop Shop (OSS). For domestic sales, use your own country's rate. The rules differ for goods, so check with your accountant if you ship products across borders.

Can I invoice in pounds or dollars?

Yes — EUR, GBP, USD, CHF and 12 further currencies are supported. Note that some tax authorities require the VAT amount to also be expressed in your national currency when you invoice in a foreign currency.

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. The form, the calculations and the PDF are all produced locally in your browser. Nothing is sent to or stored on a server, and closing the tab removes everything.

Does the PDF look professional enough for real clients?

Yes. The PDF uses a clean A4 layout with your business and client blocks, an itemised table, net/VAT/gross totals, your IBAN for payment, and the legally required VAT wording — the same structure used by established invoicing software.

How it works

Everything happens on this page, in your browser. As you type in the forms — your business details, your client’s details, and the invoice items with quantities and unit prices — the generator rebuilds the invoice in the live preview on every keystroke. Each line amount is quantity × unit price, the net total is the sum of all lines, and VAT is calculated on that net amount: at the rate you select in standard mode, or 0% in the reverse-charge and small-business-exemption modes, where the legally required note is added to the invoice automatically. If your client is a business in another EU country, the tool even detects the cross-border sale and suggests the reverse charge for you. The issue date starts as today and the due date as 14 days later, and both can be changed. When you click Download, the PDF is generated locally in your browser as a print-ready A4 document — your data is never uploaded to a server, and nothing is kept when you close the tab.

Frequently asked questions

How exactly are the totals calculated?

Each line amount is quantity × unit price. The net total is the sum of all line amounts, VAT is charged on the whole net amount (net × rate ÷ 100), and the total due is net + VAT. Amounts are shown rounded to two decimal places in your chosen currency. In reverse-charge and exemption modes the rate is 0%, so the net total and the total due are the same and the required legal note is printed on the invoice instead.

Can I give my client a discount?

There is no separate discount field. The simplest way is to lower the unit price of the item itself. Alternatively, add the discount as its own line — for example the description “Project discount”, quantity 1 and a negative unit price — and the totals will subtract it before VAT is calculated.

Can I put my own logo on the invoice?

Not at the moment — there is no logo upload. The PDF uses a fixed, clean A4 layout with your business name, address, VAT ID and IBAN shown at the top, which is what clients and accountants need in order to identify and pay the invoice.

Can I invoice a client outside the European Union?

Yes. Choose the client’s country (or “Other”) and any of the 16 currencies, including USD, GBP and CHF. For services supplied from the EU to a recipient outside the EU, VAT is generally not charged in your own country; if you set the rate to 0%, the invoice prints the note “Supply of services to a recipient outside the EU — VAT not applicable in the supplier’s country.” Check your local rules for goods and for your specific service.

I made a mistake on an invoice I already downloaded — what now?

The downloaded PDF itself cannot be edited, and nothing was submitted anywhere, so simply correct the details in the form and download a fresh PDF. If you have already sent the faulty invoice to your client, most accountants recommend issuing a corrected invoice or a credit note under a new invoice number instead of silently replacing the file — ask your accountant which applies in your country.
