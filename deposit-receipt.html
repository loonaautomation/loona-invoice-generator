Deposit Receipt

Use a deposit receipt when a customer pays part of the price in advance — to book a wedding photographer, reserve a venue, or start a building job. It proves the deposit was received and states what it is for.

What it must show

  • The business name and address, and the customer’s name
  • The date and a receipt number
  • The total price of the job and the deposit paid
  • The balance still due and when it is due
  • Whether the deposit is refundable, and on what terms

Worked example

A wedding photographer charges 2,000.00 in total. The couple pays a 50% deposit of 1,000.00 at booking. The receipt shows the total (2,000.00), the deposit (1,000.00) and the balance still due (1,000.00).

Frequently asked questions

Is a deposit refundable?

That depends on your terms and your country’s consumer law. State the position on the receipt itself — “non-refundable” or “refundable until 30 days before the date” — so there is no argument later.

What is the difference between a deposit receipt and an invoice?

A deposit receipt proves that part-payment has been received. An invoice asks for payment. When the work is done, invoice the balance and show the deposit already paid, so the customer is not charged twice.

How much deposit should I ask for?

50% is the usual figure for bookings and custom work. For larger jobs, take staged deposits tied to milestones instead of one large deposit.
