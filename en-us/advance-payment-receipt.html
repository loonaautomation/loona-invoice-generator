Advance Payment Receipt

Use an advance payment receipt when a customer pays before the work starts or the goods are delivered — an advance on a building job, a prepayment for a course, or money paid on account. It is similar to a deposit receipt, but for an advance on a running account rather than a booking deposit.

What it must show

  • The business name and address, and the customer’s name
  • The date and a receipt number
  • The total price of the work or goods
  • The advance paid and the balance still due
  • How the advance will be used (deducted from the final invoice)

Worked example

A builder’s job is priced at 1,500.00. The customer pays 500.00 in advance for materials. The receipt shows the total (1,500.00), the advance (500.00) and the balance still due (1,000.00), which will be invoiced on completion.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an advance payment and a deposit?

A deposit secures a booking and may be non-refundable; an advance is a prepayment towards the final price and is normally deducted from the final invoice. State which one it is on the receipt.

Should the advance be deducted from the final invoice?

Yes — show the advance as a line on the final invoice (“less advance received: 500.00”) so the customer is charged only the balance.

Is an advance payment taxable when I receive it?

In most countries, tax on an advance is due when the invoice is issued or the supply is made, not when the money arrives — but some countries tax advances on receipt. Check your country’s rules.
